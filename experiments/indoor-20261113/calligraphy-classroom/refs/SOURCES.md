# SOURCES — calligraphy classroom

Spatial references (links only, not checked in as images):

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Antique_Chinese_calligraphy_table_(32689388067).jpg — a broad antique writing table at the Royal Ontario Museum. Wikimedia Commons, Thomas Quine.
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chinese_calligraphy_demonstration.jpg — a demonstration sheet with brush, ink, and paper in reach of the writer. Wikimedia Commons.
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Calligraphy_lesson_(79076113).jpg — a lesson with the teacher’s sheet as the thing the class watches. Wikimedia Commons.
- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:East_Asian_calligraphy_tools — brush, ink, paper, and inkstone (文房四寶), plus seal paste. Wikimedia Commons.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Principles_of_Yong — 永字八法, the teaching character used on the center scroll and the demonstration sheet.

The room is not a reconstruction of any of these. The table and the lesson suggested a wide felt surface and a sheet the class can read. The tool category set the kit on each desk. 永 is the character being taught.
