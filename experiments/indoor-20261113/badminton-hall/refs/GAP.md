# GAP — Shuttle Hall

No badminton racket, shuttle, net, or post in the catalog. Those are procedural, scaled to the BWF limits (racket 670 × 230 mm, net 760 mm deep, posts 1.55 m). The mesh cord is drawn a little heavier than a 1–2 mm tournament cord so an 18 mm cell still reads after the toon pass and in a screenshot.

The centre sag is the legal 26 mm. It is a gentle curve; the white centre strap is what makes it readable. Optional Diagram B testing marks are not painted. Wheelchair and half-court markings are not included.

The display bookcase is still that bake: green stain, a SHUTTLES header, and tubes on the shelves. It is not a sports locker. The chalkboard bake is still that mesh; the easel feet are hidden, the frame is dark, and the face is a club score. From the gallery corner you mostly see its edge. The school desk is a sign-in counter, slightly domestic next to the benches. The flowerpot bake is empty; a small plant is built on top of it. The pendants are domestic lamps and hang only in the gallery, to mark the shoe-change threshold against the cool hall battens.

There are no players, no umpire chair, and no changing rooms. A match chair would have to sit outside the 2 m / 1.5 m clearances; the gallery benches are the seating. The hall is enclosed. The south end in the default frame is a long timber approach inside the room, not a lobby with doors of its own. The west door opens into a small vestibule. There is still no daylight.

Lines are raised meshes, not inlaid tape. The toon key is one hard shadow, not a lux study. The darkest toon step is lifted off black so a shadowed mat stays green; it is still a four-step posterize, not a soft render. The east wall takes the least light. Four canvas pads and a small notice break the plane; it is still a quiet wall beside the courts. Unlit is flat on purpose, and it is the view that shows the two wall colours without the shade. The 9.05 m clear height meets the "other sanctioned tournament" rule and the Badminton England international minimum; it is not the 12 m major-event hall, and the trusses sit at that clear height rather than higher with a separate lighting bridge.
