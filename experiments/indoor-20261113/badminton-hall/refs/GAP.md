# GAP — Shuttle Hall

No badminton racket, shuttle, net, or post in the catalog. Those are procedural, scaled to the BWF limits (racket 670 × 230 mm, net 760 mm deep, posts 1.55 m). The mesh cord is drawn a little heavier than a 1–2 mm tournament cord so an 18 mm cell still reads after the toon pass and in a screenshot.

The centre sag is the legal 26 mm. It is a gentle curve; the white centre strap is what makes it readable. Optional Diagram B testing marks are not painted. Wheelchair and half-court markings are not included.

The display bookcase is a museum case used as shuttle and grip storage, not a sports locker. The chalkboard is a school board; its original panel is hidden and a draw sheet replaces it. The school desk is a sign-in counter, slightly domestic next to the benches. The flowerpot bake is empty; a small plant is built on top of it. The pendants are domestic lamps and hang only in the gallery, to mark the shoe-change threshold against the cool hall battens.

There are no players, no umpire chair, and no changing rooms. A match chair would have to sit outside the 2 m / 1.5 m clearances; the gallery benches are the seating. The south wall is a cutaway, not a real opening. Badminton England wants no daylight, so the cut is the only break in the enclosure. The door opens onto that cut void rather than into a modelled lobby.

Lines are raised meshes, not inlaid tape. The toon key is one hard shadow, not a lux study. The darkest toon step is lifted off black so a shadowed mat stays green; it is still a four-step posterize, not a soft render. The east wall takes the least light and stays the quiet plane. Unlit is flat on purpose, and it is the view that shows the two wall colours without the shade. The 9.05 m clear height meets the "other sanctioned tournament" rule and the Badminton England international minimum; it is not the 12 m major-event hall, and the trusses sit at that clear height rather than higher with a separate lighting bridge.
