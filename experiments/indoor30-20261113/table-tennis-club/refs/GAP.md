Closed in this scene:

- Three tables at 2.74 × 1.525 × 0.76 m. The baked bounds are checked in `test_club_spec.mjs`.
- White 2 cm edge lines and a 3 mm centre line, drawn on the top, not modelled into the blue sheet.
- Mesh net 15.25 cm high, white top tape, posts 15.25 cm outside the side line, with a clamp on the top.
- Each bay is 10 × 5 m. End clearance is 3.63 m and side clearance is 1.74 m. Surrounds are 76 cm, with a 0.9 m gate on each south end.
- A backless scorer bench on the east side of each net, facing the table, with a card stand between the bench and the side line.

Remaining:

- A World Title court is about 14 × 7 m. These are club practice bays, the size Manchester TTC used for a Sunday hall, not championship run-back.
- The net is a crossed texture on a plane. It does not sag, and the mesh is not a cord.
- The 3 mm centre line is correct and easy to lose from the hall camera. The detail camera is the one that shows it.
- Scorer seats are the museum bench, which has no back. The score is painted text on a small board, not a flip-number box.
- Baskets are a fruit-basket rewrite filled with 40 mm balls. The cart is a retail dump bin. Neither is an ITTF ball box.
- The table frame is a simple welded undercarriage, not a Sponeta or Butterfly mechanism. The blue sheet is a flat colour, not a pimpled rubber texture.
- The default frame is the three courts. The corner frame is the gallery: baskets, the ball cart, the paddle rack, and the duty desk, outside the 10 × 5 m bays. The scorer benches stay on the east side of each net.
- No players. Grounding is a hard directional shadow. Ceiling joists do not cast, so the courts are not crossed by a beam grid.
