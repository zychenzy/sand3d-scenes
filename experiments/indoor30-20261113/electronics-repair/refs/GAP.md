# Gaps

- No catalog GLB reads as a stereo microscope or a fume-extractor arm. The compound microscope on Hugging Face looks like a science-lab scope, and the Bofa-style extractor is a featureless box. Both are procedural here: two eyepieces on a stand, and a labeled FUME box with a jointed arm. They are stylized solids.
- The bench supply, oscilloscope, parts cabinet, phone tray, intake counter, ready cubbies, and keycap cart are procedural for the same reason. A blank beige "DC supply" bake and slab laptops were rejected after candidate renders. The cart uses rows of keycaps, not a keyboard slab.
- The curved cowork monitor was left out so the room does not read as an esports desk. The test bench carries the scope trace and the supply instead.
- The multimeter bake includes probe leads. `prepareAsset` scales the whole bounding box, so the red body stays small next to the scope. The probes are the honest part of that mesh.
- The soldering-iron bake includes the cord and wall plug. It lies on the mat beside the fume box. There is no iron stand in the catalog.
- The workbench GLB is a tall back panel plus a walnut top. Scaled to 1.5 m overall, the work surface lands near 0.74 m. Dining chairs (0.88 m overall) face that top; the staff stool faces the 1.04 m counter from the shop side.
- Drawer labels (0402, 0603, 0805, 1206, CAP, RES, and the rest) follow the SMD sizes in the soldering photos. No usable photo of a labeled small-parts cabinet turned up, so the cabinet itself is not matched to a reference image.
- Contact shadows under chairs are discs. The toon path has no screen-space AO. Wainscot, floor tiles, and the ceiling do not cast, which keeps the walls from picking up shadow acne. The ceiling is a full soffit; the shop lights and beams hang on its underside.
- The multimeter stands on the solder bench beside the phone board so the red body and the green display read in the detail shot. The probe leads still set the mesh height, so the body stays small. The stereo head is two white eyepieces over a short objective, not a compound lab microscope.
- Window lettering is canvas text on planes. It is readable at the default camera and disappears if the plane faces the wall.
