# Railway dining car

A long restaurant carriage, shown as a cutaway from the aisle side.

Zones, in order from the -Z end: galley (service counter, pass, tighter high windows), saloon (four bays), vestibule (luggage cubby, steps, gangway door).

The saloon has two long tables along the window wall. Each table seats two solid-plinth benches against the windows and two opposite them, so the benches face across the top. Seat front is local +Z (the baked backrest is on -Z): rotation π/2 faces +X and rotation -π/2 faces -X.

Each dining top is long along the carriage. One pedestal spine runs that length. The ends are bracketed back to the window wall, clear of the benches. The galley counter is a separate wall-mounted surface. There is no four-leg square table.

Saloon windows are individual openings on a 1.8 m pitch (about 1.05 m of glass, 0.75 m pier) with a mullion, hopper bar, sill, and curtains that are not all drawn the same way. The middle window is a cross-aisle, not another copy of the table. Galley windows are smaller, higher, and on a shorter pitch. Luggage racks run above the windows and along the cutaway side; cases sit on some of the rack, not all of it. Warm practicals: two pendants over each long table, a sconce on each pier, a heat lamp at the pass.

Palette: tuscan body, cream interior, walnut tops, burgundy carpet, brass lamps. Toon hard, four-step gradient, hard shadows, emissive bulbs, contact discs under seats and tables.

Units: metres.
