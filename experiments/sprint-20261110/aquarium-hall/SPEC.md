# Aquarium visitor hall

A small public aquarium gallery, not a domestic room. One near floor-to-ceiling planted tank is the reason for the visit. A second zone is a niche walk of three smaller live exhibits. Circulation enters through the open cutaway, passes two benches that face the glass, and can peel left along the niches.

## Room

12.0 × 8.4 × 4.0 m. Back wall and left wall only (cutaway). Units metres.

## Zones

1. **Kelp forest tank** — low curb, mullioned glass about 10 m wide and 3.4 m tall, sand, stacked rocks, ribbon kelp, broad leaves, driftwood, three large fish at different depths, and a school of about thirty tetras. Teal point lights sit in front of the glass and spill onto the floor and benches. A caustic decal lies on the floor. A rail keeps visitors off the glass. The curb carries the tank title.
2. **Niche walk** — three different openings on the left wall (garibaldi, señorita, rockfish), each with its own rock or kelp, one fish, glass, and a plaque. Visitor-height interpretation panels stand between the niches. Floor strips mark the aisle and the left walk. An information desk sits at the right of the entry.

## Furniture

- Two shared museum benches, both `rotationY = π/2`, long axis parallel to the glass so a sitter faces the tank. Seat height 0.46 m.
- Two shared flowerpots with procedural foliage stand clear of the tank, one at each end of the rail.
- Lectern, stanchion rail, and information desk are built for this hall.

## Light

Dim slate room, hard key from the open side, and a teal spill from the tank onto the floor and benches. A subtle caustic decal sits on the floor in front of the glass. No bloom, no outline. Emissive caps on the soffit spots and on the desk lamp. Unlit mode keeps the same colors, with glow mixed into the flat color so the tank, caustics, and wayfinding strips still read.

## Palette

Slate walls and floor, sand and brass as the warm notes, teal water, one saturated orange fish as the accent.
