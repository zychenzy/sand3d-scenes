# Gaps — mountain overlook

What the references show that this scene does not reproduce, and what was built instead.

## Scale

Stegastein is about 30 m long and 4 m wide, 650 m above the fjord. This deck is about 8.6 m from the stone abutment to the glass end, widening from 2.6 m to 3.6 m. A 30 m deck would hide the switchback and the shelter in the same camera. The cantilever is still the focal object: the tip stands about 10 m above the dropped ground.

## Structure

The real Stegastein deck is a deep laminated-timber and steel cantilever with most of the anchorage hidden in the hillside, and a glass pane at the outer end. This model shows three splayed timber beams, cross joists, plank decking, and two thin steel straps back to a stone abutment. The straps are there so the beams can stay slender. There is no glass floor and no hidden steel ladder frame. Beam depth is 0.40 m, kept short of a full-size glulam so the underside does not turn into a stack of oversized beams.

## Stair

Moro Rock is a 350-step concrete-and-stone stair on a bare granite dome, with pipe rails and almost no soil. This trail is a shorter stone switchback cut into a grass shoulder, with landings at the turns and a topographic outcrop that the path bends around. Risers stay at or under 0.16 m. Only the upper exposed edge has a thin rail; the lower flights use the cut bank and a few dry-stone retainers.

## Shelter

The Skjerdingvatnet gapahuk is a specific measured building. This one is a modest plank shelter of the same type: three walls, open front facing the view, bench against the back wall, turf roof, stone pads under the posts. It is not a survey of that shelter. The fire ring has cold logs only — no flame and no point light.

## Ground and plants

There is no heightfield or scan of Aurland, Sequoia, or Hurricane Ridge. The shoulder, cliff, and three peak ranges are authored. Pines are built from trunks, uneven branches, and flat needle tufts; one snag and three krummholz mats break the set. Grasses are clumps, not a repeated card. The Hurricane Ridge photo used for the meadow is daytime, so the sunrise grade is invented from the brief rather than matched to that file.

## Bench

The viewing seat is the existing sand3d museum bench (backless oak slab, curved steel legs), scaled slightly deeper and turned so the seat depth faces the peaks. It is a contemporary bench on a timber deck, not a timber bench built with the deck. A second, simpler plank bench is inside the shelter.
