# Sources — seaside promenade

Public photographs used for composition, materials, and scale. No reference images are stored in git. All four were opened and inspected before the scene was built.

## 1. Curved stone sea wall, rocks, and bright water

[Beachfront, Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beachfront,_Promenade_des_Anglais,_Nice,_France.jpg)

- File: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Beachfront%2C_Promenade_des_Anglais%2C_Nice%2C_France.jpg`
- Subhashish Panigrahi, 29 June 2016. CC BY-SA 4.0.
- Used for: a stone wall meeting a rocky point, steps down to the beach, turquoise shallows against deeper blue, and bright high cloud.

## 2. Bend of a stone promenade wall

[Bend in the Cobb](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bend_in_the_Cobb_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1989744.jpg)

- File: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Bend_in_the_Cobb_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1989744.jpg`
- Dylan Moore, Geograph, CC BY-SA 2.0.
- Used for: the wall bending in plan, large coursed stone, a level paved walk, and a mast against a bright cloudy sky.

## 3. Steps built into the wall

[Cobb — Steps Onto The Cobb](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cobb_-_Steps_Onto_The_Cobb_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1599557.jpg)

- File: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Cobb_-_Steps_Onto_The_Cobb_-_geograph.org.uk_-_1599557.jpg`
- Chris Talbot, Geograph, CC BY-SA 2.0.
- Used for: pale stone treads, darker coursed cheeks, and the way a flight is built as part of the wall rather than placed on top of it.

## 4. Marram clumps on open sand

[Ammophila arenaria on a dune, Westenschouwen](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ammophila_arenaria_(Marram_grass)_on_dune,_Westenschouwen,_the_Netherlands.jpg)

- File: `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Ammophila_arenaria_%28Marram_grass%29_on_dune%2C_Westenschouwen%2C_the_Netherlands.jpg`
- Bj.schoenmakers, 15 September 2012. CC0.
- Used for: grass as separate leaning clumps of mixed height, with bare sand between them, not a continuous hedge.
