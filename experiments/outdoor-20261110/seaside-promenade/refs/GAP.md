# Gaps — seaside promenade

The scene is a small authored place, not a survey of one beach.

- The Cobb photographs show a high harbour wall and a steep side stair. This promenade uses a lower parapet you can see over, and broad tiers down to the sand. The stone coursing, the bend, and the built-in cheeks come from those photos; the heights do not.
- Nice shows a pebble beach, a tall castle wall, and a crowded shore. Only the rocky left end, the water colours, and the idea of steps down to the beach are taken from it. There is no hotel front, road, or crowd.
- No reference in the set is a small timber viewing deck. The boardwalk pocket is composed so there is a second place to stand over the beach. Plank width is about 0.28 m. It stops on the sand; it is not a pier.
- Sandsend’s kiosk is a closed timber hut. The gelato counter, dusty awning stripes, tubs, and sign are authored. The hut stays under 2.6 m wide and about 2.7 m to the ridge.
- No reference shows a bicycle stand. The three hoops are ordinary Sheffield stands, with two bikes.
- Catalog seating is the indoor backless museum bench (X-frame legs), not a promenade bench with a back. It is placed with its long side along the wall so one face looks out to sea.
- The flowerpots are the cafe-kit pot, used as two planters beside the kiosk. Dune grass, rocks, sails, the wall, and the water are procedural. Nothing in the local bakes covers those.
- Water is banded geometry plus a broken foam line and a few flat glints. It does not simulate waves, refraction, or a tide line that moves.
- Distant sails are silhouettes at three sizes. There is no rigging detail and no harbour.
- The far side of the bay is open water under a clear sky. There is no headland mesh. A solid headland read as a hovering blob, so the horizon stays empty.
