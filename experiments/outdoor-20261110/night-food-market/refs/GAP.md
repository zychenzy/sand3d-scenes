# Gaps — night food market

The three Commons photos are a long crowded street, one noodle booth, and a yatai row. This scene keeps a four-stall length so the default camera can read the whole place.

**Taken from the photos**
- Stall row under separate fabric awnings, bulbs at the front edge, paper lanterns over the lane.
- Noodle stall: stock pots, bowl stack, ladle, pale menu board with 牛肉麵 / 牛雜湯 / 乾麵 / 滷蛋 and prices.
- Grill: charcoal bed, grate, skewers, tongs. Drinks: urn, cup stack, citrus. Produce: crates of cabbage, tomato, scallion, mango, hanging scale, price cards.
- Service lane behind the counters with gas cylinders, bins, and delivery crates. Vendor side of each stall has a shelf, stool or chest, and a hose into the noodle stall.
- Communal tables in front of the counters, not inside the walking aisle. A wider gap between the noodle stall and the tea stall is the staff cut through to the lane.

**Honest gaps**
- No people, scooters, or crowd. The Raohe street photo is packed; this site would look empty beside it.
- Catalog furniture is wooden dining chairs and trestle/square tables, not the red plastic stools and folding metal tables in the noodle photo. Chairs are turned to face their table.
- No baked wok, skewer, crate, lantern, or gas-cylinder mesh. Those are procedural.
- Sign copy is real dish names and prices in Traditional Chinese plus short English stall names. It is not a reproduction of any one photographed menu, and it is not shop-front advertising from the wide Raohe frame (watches, clinics).
- Night lighting is deliberately below the clipped tungsten bulbs in the photos so highlights stay under control. Warmth is in the awnings, lantern color, and fill, not in spotlight discs.
- One street tree and one narrow back tree. The references are mostly built street, not a garden.
