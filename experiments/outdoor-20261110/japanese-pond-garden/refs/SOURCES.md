# Sources

These are the photographs used for composition, proportions, and materials. The files are not stored in git. Each page was opened and the image itself was viewed before anything here was written.

## 1. Timber arch, stone abutment, water, lilies

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Traditional_wooden_bridge_in_a_Japanese_Garden.jpg
- Author: Simonilja
- Date: 21 August 2022
- License: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0)
- Place: Japanese Garden, Wrocław

What the picture actually shows, and what was taken from it: a dark weathered timber arch, gentle rather than a semicircular moon bridge. Vertical posts with small rounded caps, a top rail and a mid rail that follow the arch, one timber pile in the water under the crown, and a rubble-stone abutment where the deck meets the bank. The water is green-brown, not pure blue. Lily pads sit in a cluster at the near edge, not as a carpet. Dense trees and a weeping willow frame the bridge.

## 2. Stepping stones and a rocky bank

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kiyosumi-5.JPG
- Author: Irina Gelbukh
- License: [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0)
- Place: Kiyosumi Teien, Tokyo
- The file description is only "Kiyosumi Teien". The image is a curving line of large, flat-topped, irregular stones leaving a rocky grassy shore and stepping into green water, with pines and a distant pavilion.

Taken from it: stone sizes and stride gaps vary; the tops are worn and pale, the wet bases darker; the bank is a jumble of rocks and grass tufts, not a curb; the near water is shallow enough to read the bed.

## 3. Stroll-garden layout

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ritsurin_Garden.jpg
- Author: Mason tang photography
- Date: 9 July 2018, 13:29
- License: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0)
- Place: Ritsurin Garden, Takamatsu

What the picture shows: an elevated view of a large stroll garden. An arched timber bridge is the midground focal point over an irregular pond. Pine branches frame the foreground. Wooded hills close the background. Small buildings sit off to the side and do not cover the bridge. The land is asymmetric.

Taken from it: bridge as the focal point rather than a full-frame object, pond wider than the span, pines kept distinct from the maples, foreground framing, and a hill instead of a back wall. The photograph's pines are cloud-pruned pads; the scene uses tiered needle tufts so the trunks do not read as bare poles with discs.
