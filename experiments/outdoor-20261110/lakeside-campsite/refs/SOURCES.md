# SOURCES — lakeside campsite

Three public photographs. No reference images are stored in git. Composition notes are what this scene actually took from each frame.

## 1. Hyatt Lake campground, Cascade-Siskiyou, Oregon

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyatt_Lake_camping_-_50109614512.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4d/Hyatt_Lake_camping_-_50109614512.jpg
- Author: Bureau of Land Management Oregon and Washington (Kyle Sullivan), 21 June 2020
- License: [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/)

Used for the layered conifer shore: a tall foreground pine with a visible trunk beside a lower, uneven treeline, a rocky and grassy water edge, and open water with small surface texture. The photo is a distant midday view across the lake, not a close campsite.

## 2. Cracker Lake campground, Glacier National Park

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cracker_Lake_Campground.jpg
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bd/Cracker_Lake_Campground.jpg
- Author: Paul Barta, 9 September 2019
- License: [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/)

Used for a single small tent on a worn pad set back from a curving shore, and for water that changes from a pale edge to a darker body. The site is alpine meadow, not pine forest, and the tent is far from the camera.

## 3. Birkenhead Lake campground, seen from the dock

- https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Birkenhead_lake.JPG
- Direct file: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bb/Birkenhead_lake.JPG
- Author: Orlandkurtenbach, 6 August 2006
- License: public domain (CC0-style release on Wikimedia Commons)

Used for the view from the water toward a mixed conifer shore: narrow spires, broader trees, and at least one bare trunk, with the lake occupying the foreground. The caption says the photo is taken from the dock. The dock structure itself is not in the frame.
