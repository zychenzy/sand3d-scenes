# Gaps

What the rendered plot does not do, after looking at the four shots.

- Leaves are bent planes, not scanned plants. Canes are straight cylinders. Bean vines are thin tubes, so the close view shows a lot of bare cane, the way the Easton row does while the plants are still climbing.
- Easton Lodge is a brick walled garden. This plot has a timber and wire fence. The wall is not reproduced.
- The Cambridge shed photo has a pile of scrap sheet and a leaning pallet. The shed here is weathered board and a corrugated roof, without that junk.
- The water butt is a ribbed open cylinder with a water disc in the top. It is not a scanned barrel. The water reads from above more clearly than from the side.
- Houses are plain masses: walls, a roof, a chimney, and dark windows. No brick course, no gardens of their own.
- The picnic-end seats are the indoor ladder-back dining chair. They face the table. They are not garden benches.
- The mug on the table is the cafe cup, orange sleeve included.
- No people, no wheelbarrow, no soil displacement. Compost is a mound inside slatted bays.
- Most foliage receives shadows and does not cast them, so leaf shadows stay thin. Beds, canes, the shed, the picnic table, and the trees do cast.
- The narrow tree east of the fence is a trunk with a loose dark crown. It is not a dense conifer.
- Distant ground fades into a pale morning fog. The fog is lighter than the grass, so the horizon is a soft band.
