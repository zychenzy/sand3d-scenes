# Gaps

The photographs are daytime. Tsurunoyu is a snowy February afternoon, Shinhodaka is autumn, and Takanoyu is a green midday. This scene is blue hour because that was the brief, so snow, ice, and saturated summer greens are not in the model.

No people. Depth is carried by the seat, the floor, the breaker boulder, and the bucket.

Water is a tinted surface over a modelled floor. It is not a milky fluid simulation and it does not refract. The mineral colour is darkened for dusk; it is not the opaque pale turquoise of the winter Tsurunoyu bath and not a swimming-pool blue.

Steam is a few soft cards over the spout, not a volume. Opacity stays low so the pool stays readable.

The three main frames show forest, reeds, and bare winter trees. A bamboo grove is not in those frames. It is here as the privacy screen the brief asked for: segmented culms and drooping leaves, not identical lollipops. Reeds follow the Shinhodaka bank grasses more closely.

Rocks, timber, and mountains are procedural. The catalog bakes that fit are a flowerpot (entry planter) and a thermos (flask on the deck). There is no onsen tub, lantern, or bamboo mesh in the baked set used here. An industrial pendant and the museum bench were skipped because they fight the timber shelter.

The overflow is a stone swale at roughly 1:15, then a short cascade off the retaining wall. It is not a trapped modern drain. The bridge is a low plank crossing, not an arched garden bridge.

Toon hard banding limits soft dusk gradients. The ramp starts above black so shadows stay readable. Lanterns are emissive paper plus short point lights; if a light still reads as a disc, the paper colour is doing the real work.

The mountain masses are faceted ridges in fog, not a specific peak from the photos.
